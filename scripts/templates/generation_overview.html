How to read this overview

Every registry person appears once in the counts. The largest connected family is the reference for numbered generations; ties use the lowest person ID. Partners share a generation, parents are one generation earlier, and children are one generation later. Placement follows connections in both directions, so shorter ancestry branches align with the family they join. The earliest level in this connected family is Gen 1. Disconnected people appear under “Unaligned”; their position relative to the reference family is unknown. If relationships imply contradictory positions, the entire affected connected family is marked as conflicting and left unaligned. Alternative accounts are retained without choosing one. These are family positions, not historical cohorts or the independent ancestry depths used in analytics.

Birth-year ranges show the earliest and latest recorded birth years among all people in each generation. The stacked bars show people with known birth years in dark green and unknown birth years in pale green. Hover, focus, or tap either portion to see its count. Total bar height includes both portions. A single known year is shown once; “Dates unknown” means no birth years are recorded. Ranges can overlap across generations and stay fixed when you search or limit surname rows. No missing dates are estimated, and dates do not change family-based generation placement.

Rows use the registry’s family-name groups, including recorded spelling variants. A shared surname does not establish a connected branch. “Unknown” remains its own group. Search covers group names and recorded surnames and ignores accents. The generation bars, Unaligned column, and collection totals together include everyone; the visible-row subtotal follows the search and row limit. Colors use one fixed scale across all groups.
