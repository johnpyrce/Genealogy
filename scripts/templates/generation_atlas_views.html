How to read these views

Every registry person appears exactly once in each view. Generation placements reuse the alignment computed from the family registries: the largest connected family provides the numbered generations, whose earliest level is Gen 1. Partners share a generation, parents are one generation earlier, and children are one generation later. Vertical order is a depth-first walk of family unions — partners are placed in adjacent rows and children follow their parents — so sibling clusters stay together. Row position carries no meaning beyond this grouping. People with no established position appear in the Unaligned column (Labels and Dots) or below the separator (Lifelines); no position is invented for them.

Labels draws every name with recorded birth years; scroll in any direction. Dots draws no labels so the whole family fits on one screen; the slider controls dot size. Lifelines encodes time on the x axis, divided into nine vertical blocks alternating between a very light beige and the plain background, split at each generation’s earliest recorded birth year, with an end mark at the current year. Solid lines in surname-group colours run from recorded birth to recorded death with a dot at each recorded date. Dashed terracotta lines belong to people without both dates: a standard 40-year span anchored at the recorded date, marked by a single dot and shortened when it would run past the current year. People with no recorded dates keep the dashed dot-free style and are centred on the recorded birth-year range of their generation — the position reflects the generation’s dates, never the person’s own. Only people with neither dates nor an established generation appear in the side lane. The slider controls line and date-dot size in this view.

Colours mark the largest family-name groups; all remaining groups use grey. Selecting a legend entry highlights that group and dims everything else in every view.
