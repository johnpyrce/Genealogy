# The Pyrć Name

The chart *Gościńscy z Wapiennego* records the surname as **Pyrć**. The Polish Academy of Sciences surname dictionary treats **Pyrc** as a variant in the **Pyrcz** name group. 
[Polish Academy of Sciences surname dictionary](https://nazwiska.ijppan.pl/haslo/show/name/PYRC), [Forebears: Pyrc](https://forebears.io/surnames/pyrc)

## Derivation

The Polish Academy of Sciences gives two plausible explanations:

- **Polish nickname origin:** from the dialect verb *pyrczeć*, meaning **“to bubble or gurgle while cooking.”** It may originally have described someone by a characteristic sound, habit, or figurative association.
- **Germanic personal-name origin:** from German **Pertz**, a shortened form of compound names containing *Bercht/Berht*, meaning **“bright” or “shining.”**

The geographic distribution suggests that separate Pyrc families could have arisen independently—some from a Polish dialect nickname and others through Polish adaptation of a German name. [Polish Academy of Sciences surname dictionary](https://nazwiska.ijppan.pl/haslo/show/name/PYRC)

## Pronunciation

Approximately:

- **Pyrc:** *pirts*

More precisely, Polish **y** is a short central vowel somewhat between the vowels in English *bit* and *put*, while final **c** sounds like **ts**. It is one syllable and does **not** rhyme exactly with English “pierce” or “perks.”

## Male and female forms

Modern Polish usage is:

- man: **Jan Pyrc**
- woman: **Anna Pyrc**
- family: **rodzina Pyrców**—“the Pyrc family”

## Modern distribution

Counts vary by source and date. Forebears currently lists 265 people named Pyrc in Poland and estimates 336 worldwide; its related-name list includes Pyrć and Pyrcz. These figures are useful indicators of rarity and geography, not evidence that all people with these spellings share a common ancestor. [Forebears: Pyrc](https://forebears.io/surnames/pyrc), [Forebears: Pyrcz](https://forebears.io/surnames/pyrcz)

The spellings Pyrć, Pyrc, and Pyrcz can appear as related variants in Polish records.
