# The Pyrce Name

The American family of Joseph and Sophie came to use the name **Pyrce**, while Polish records in this collection use **Pyrć** for Joseph/Józef.

"Pyrce" is a rare name.

It's usually a variant spelling of the English Pearce or Pierce, which come from the medieval given name Piers, the Norman French form of Peter (Latin Petrus, "rock"). The "y" spelling looks older or more archaic, and it's much less common today than Pierce or Pearce. It's mostly found in English-speaking countries, with roots in England and Wales.

We know this family
"Pyrce" is a respelling of Pyrc or Pyrcz. In Polish, "c" is pronounced "ts" and "cz" is pronounced "ch," so Pyrc sounds like "Pirts" and Pyrcz like "Pirch." 
A clerk or family writing by ear could produce Pyrch from Pyrcz. 
Pyrce looks like a later step, where the family made the name look more English and closer to Pierce. That kind of drift is very common in immigrant families. A formal or informal name change in the 1930s wouldn't be unusual either.

Which is exactly the case.
A 1933 [Petition for Citizenship](<../../media/SophieJoseph/1933 - Citizenship Petition.png>) for Sophie records
records Sophie's surname as **Pyrch**.

Sophie became a naturalized U.S. citizen on March 22, 1938, five years after the petition. The reverse of the [naturalization record](<../../media/SophieJoseph/1938 - Naturalization - Back.tif>) records a name change to **Sophie Pyrce**.

Later records for Sophie and her children use **Pyrce**.
