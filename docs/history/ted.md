# Ted Pyrce and the Hewitt B-29 Superfortress crew 

![Hewitt B-29 crew](../../media/Ted/1940s%20-%20Original%20WWII%20Crew%20Lost%20in%20Japan.jpg)
**The Hewitt B-29 crew assembed and trained in Pyote, Texas "Rattlesnake Base".  Ted Pyrce is first row second from left, Hewitt
is second row first from left, Appleby is second row third.**

Ted Pyrce joined the Army Air Force in World War II and trained to be the radio operator on a B-29 Superfortress crew.
He was added to the crew
commanded by 1st Lieutenant James Hewitt, Pilot and Commander in Pyote, Texas where the crew was
assembled and completed their training
before being sent to the Pacific theatre.
He was 22 years old at the time.

Ted became ill with influenza just as the crew was to be deployed to the Pacific, and was so sick that he required hospitalization.
Since the crew was scheduled to leave, he was replaced by one of the radio operator trainers, T/Sgt. William Andrews, who
had recently returned from serving in Europe.

When Ted recovered, he became an instructor and served in that capacity until his military service was
complete.  He never left the US.
![Ted Pyrce portrait.](../../media/Ted/1940s%20-%20Aviator.jpg)

The Hewitt crew was stationed on Guam in the Mariana Islands in the Pacific, which was a base for air operations against
Japan.  They were part of the 28th Bomber Squadron, 19th Bomber Group.  The war was almost over by the time
that they arrived, and Japanese air defenses
were degraded.

On July 27, 1945, B-29A 42-94098 with the Hewitt crew of twelve took off for a bombing mission to Omuta, Japan.
The raid consisted of 350 B-29s.  Shortly after dropping their bombs, they were hit by flak and possibly attacked
by Japanese Zero fighters.  The crew abandoned the damaged bomber, which broke into several pieces and
crashed into a mountainous area.  Several crew members were killed in the crash or immediately after.
Although this was a rural area, all of the survivors were captured by the KempehTai, the Japanese military police,
within a few days and sent to the prison camp
at Fukuoka.  They were the only plane that was lost in this raid.

Here was the crew roster:

- 1st Lt. James E. Hewitt — Pilot
- F/O Gerald E. Boleyn — Co-Pilot
- F/O Charles S. Appleby — Engineer / Navigator
- 1st Lt. John W. Gothie — Bombardier / Navigator
- 2nd Lt. Wayne A. Whitely — Radar Operator / Navigator / Bombardier
- S/Sgt. Ben Thornton — Flight Engineer / Radio Operator / Gunner
- T/Sgt. William N. Andrews — Radio Operator / Radar Observer / Gunner
- Cpl. Robert J. Zancker — Gunner
- Cpl. Martin W. O'Brien Jr. — Gunner (Central Fire Control)
- Cpl. Frederick A. Stearns — Gunner
- Cpl. Robert R. Sawdye — Tail Gunner
- Capt. Louis W. Nelson — Observer

Very shortly after the crew was captured, the US dropped Hiroshima and Nagasaki with atomic bombs, and Japan surrendered.

The treatment of war prisoners by the Japanese in World War II was brutal. The Japanese
military held a cultural belief that surrendering to
an enemy was dishonorable.  US airmen were also considered to be war criminals because of
the bombing of civilian areas with incendiary bombs, causing extensive civilian casualties.

The surviving B-29 crew members were executed by their Japanese captors in two groups with gruesome methods
including decapitation.  This was done at the direction of the camp commander, and was not approved
by the Japanese military command. One group was executed before the Japanese surrender, the second group after the surrender 
to disguise what had been done.

Charles Appleby, the Navigator, was 21 when he died.  He had married in
high school as a teenager and already
had a young daughter.  He carried on an extensive correspondence with his wife, Marilyn, and other
friends and family members while enlisted in the military.  These were the basis of a book,
[When the Letters Stopped](https://www.amazon.com/When-Letters-Stopped-navigator-Charles/dp/1737740176/ref=sr_1_1?crid=NRGI5R11J834&dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.Oe--8m1p-T40R6OjOAIovBDNnqdNRFEpd2_nnNoR4avn7ZVdo7_YdX3jorjf3bTVVD9nI5Ih-a_cGCzXOo4VAeUWTSiHTqJ_MxF4JXcNtfg.vFXwY2_QRjgu4Cz55Tg6J56C0gZP08tDOR7watVZsok&dib_tag=se&keywords=when%2Bthe%2Bletters%2Bstopped&qid=1795456037&sprefix=When%2Bthe%2Bletters%2B%2Caps%2C290&sr=8-1&th=1), by Heather Buchanan, a
descendant of Appleby.  Besides the correspondence, the book
is the result of extensive research into the time, accounts
of survivors, and testimony from Japanese records and war crime trials after the war.  This book contains
a remarkably detailed account focused on Appleby and his friends and relatives, including a recreation
of what their experiences in Japan might have been like. Ted appears as a minor character.
